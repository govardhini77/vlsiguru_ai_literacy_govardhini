# Week 01 - AI Literacy Journal

## What I Worked On

This week I worked on the basic AI concepts and learned how AI can be used as a learning and engineering tool.

I studied the differences between AI, Machine Learning, Deep Learning, Generative AI, and AI agents. I also looked at how traditional rule-based software is different from AI-based systems.

I worked through questions about LLMs, AI hallucinations, AI assistants, search engines, AI agents, and different types of AI tasks such as prediction, classification, and generation.

## What I Tried

I used AI assistants to understand technical topics and compared some of the answers with Google Search and reliable technical sources.

For the hallucination experiment, I asked two AI assistants the same factual question and checked their answers against an authoritative source.

I also compared an AI assistant, Google Search, and an Intel technical reference for the question about SRAM and DRAM.

## What I Observed

I noticed that AI can explain technical topics quickly and in simple language. However, an answer can sound correct without automatically being a reliable source.

I also noticed that Google Search and an AI assistant serve different purposes. An AI assistant gives a generated explanation, while a search engine helps find different sources. The actual source still needs to be checked for reliability.

## What I Verified

I verified important technical claims using reliable sources such as IBM, NIST, Intel, Google, Spotify, and Apple sources.

The verification work helped me understand why important technical information should not be accepted only because an AI assistant gives a confident answer.

## What I Understood

I understood that AI is a broad field and that Machine Learning, Deep Learning, and Generative AI are related concepts but are not exactly the same.

I also understood the basic idea of how an LLM processes a prompt and generates a response using tokens and next-token prediction.

Most importantly, I understood that AI should be treated as a useful tool for helping with a task, while the final information still needs to be checked when accuracy is important.

## What Still Confuses Me

I still want to learn more about how AI agents actually decide which tools to use and how they handle multiple steps in a task.

I also want to understand more about how these AI concepts can be applied to VLSI and Design Verification in later weeks.

## One Thing I Will Verify Differently Next Time

Next time I use an AI assistant for a technical question, I will not immediately assume that the answer is correct. I will first understand the problem, check the important assumptions, look for reliable evidence, and test the result when possible.

## Week 01 Takeaway

My main takeaway from this week is that getting an answer from AI is only the first step. Understanding, checking, and verifying the answer are also part of the work.
